## Fundamental analysis of the coffee price

**The global coffee market is in a period of high volatility** due to a complex combination of macroeconomic tensions, logistical delays in Brazil, and growing climate risks associated with the El Niño phenomenon.

On June 18, 2026, the signing of a memorandum of understanding between the United States and Iran brought 60 days of temporary relief to energy prices. However, the resumption of attacks on July 7 reactivated tensions in the energy sector, consolidating expectations of **persistent inflation at the global level.**

The increase in initial margin requirements reduced market liquidity, triggering margin calls and forcing funds to close positions quickly, which amplified price movements. In addition, the price gap (arbitrage) between Arabica and Robusta widened notably.

Although harvesting accelerated in July, the pace of Arabica sales and exports remains below the historical average.

Despite the delay, the rains did not affect total volume and **an exceptional harvest is expected in Brazil** that would guarantee a global surplus. Additionally, the mid-July exemption from the new 25% tariff imposed by the U.S. on Brazilian instant coffee could give an extra boost to its exports.

### Impact by region:

- In **Brazil** it can cause higher temperatures during winter, reducing the probability of frost, and during flowering it can cause very high temperatures that could affect it considerably.

- In **Vietnam**, below-average rainfall forced a marginal reduction in harvest projections.

- In **Indonesia**, there is concern about the impact of dry weather during the development phase of the 27/28 season crop.

- In **Central America**, irregular rainfall patterns are being recorded, while in **Colombia** the weather remains close to average, although El Niño could threaten the Mitaca harvest.

Generally, years with an active El Niño are positive for coffee production, but when the phenomenon intensifies and becomes very strong, as is forecast, it can cause considerable damage to the harvests of the main producing countries. For this reason it is essential to monitor the progress of El Niño in order to anticipate coffee price movements.

In the short term, NOAA maintains its El Niño Advisory with **97% persistence**, and for now there is no frost forecast for any producing region of Brazil. This situation may cause a liquidation of speculative longs that were held only as frost cover, as August advances without cold.

This leaves a market with a bearish or heavy-sideways bias, with short rebounds from some weather anomaly, and growing pressure as the big wave of new coffee comes in at the end of July and in August.

A market with little money inside is a light market: it moves more on less. The base reading is a sideways week, with abrupt jumps due to lack of volume rather than real news.

With speculators at 10,583 net long against an all-time high of 144,732, there are almost no longs to liquidate — that is, little fuel to fall — and there is enormous room to buy if a trigger appears.

If a weather alert, an inventory figure or any supply noise occurs, it can set off a rapid rise, because the funds would have to rush in. If nothing happens, pressure from the Brazilian harvest keeps the ceiling in place and Arabica falls asleep in its range.

## Technical analysis of the coffee price

Since October 2025, coffee had been making progressively lower highs — 338.25 in February, 319.50 in March, 306.25 in April, 297.20 in May, 269.40 in early June — which is the classic signature of a healthy downtrend. The rally to 357.00 took out all those highs at once, generating the first higher high since October of last year.

That, technically, is a break of structure: the medium-term downtrend is no longer in force. This is confirmed by the weekly MACD, which crossed upward.

### Relative Strength Index (RSI)

Today the RSI stands at 53.16 daily, 54.02 weekly and 52.16 monthly: all three practically at the midpoint. This matters because it means the market has no accumulated tension in either direction. There is no overbought condition forcing a correction, nor oversold condition forcing a bounce.

Technically, coffee has free fuel to move in either direction, and for that reason the trigger for the next move will come from the break of the price range, not from the indicator.

### Fibonacci retracements

The recovery rally, from 242.70 to 357.00, gives retracements at 329.8 (23.6%), 313.15 (38.2%), 299.7 (50%) and 286.25 (61.8%). They show that **coffee closed at 313.80 — that is, practically nailed to the 38.2% retracement at 313.15.**

This is the shallowest retracement considered normal after a strong impulse, and the market stopping there is a constructive sign: buyers have not allowed the correction to deepen. If it loses that level in a sustained way, the next natural target is 299.7 and then 286.25, which is the limit where the July rebound would cease to have technical validity.

## Possible scenarios for next week

### Neutral scenario — Probability: 43%

- The Brazilian harvest keeps running fast and there is no frost on the radar, but certified inventories at 311,317 bags and not a single bag pending certification keep the price from falling; the two forces cancel each other out and neither wins.

- There is very little money inside the market. Open interest stood at 220,681 lots, some 52,000 fewer than six weeks ago, and the funds did not move. A market like this moves in jumps, but without direction, because nobody is betting heavily on anything.

- Coffee stays trapped between 306.40 and 323.22, sitting right on top of its 200-day moving average. Every attempt to rise to 320–323 meets sellers, and every drop to 307–309 brings out a buyer. It is an even contest.

- The week closes between 307 and 321, with the center near 314. The rains looming for Wednesday the 29th give a one-day scare, the market digests it, and the fight to define the range is postponed another week.

### Bearish scenario — Probability: 36%

- The big wave of new coffee arrives. Harvesting moves from 63% to around 72–75% and the market finally begins to price in what is already a fact: Brazil's record harvest of 71.9 million bags and a global Arabica surplus close to 9.5 million bags.

- Bags pending certification appear after several weeks at zero. That is the key signal: it means physical coffee is coming back to the exchange and it removes the only real support the price had.

- With no frost forecast, funds that remained long only as insurance against cold release the position as August approaches with nothing to fear. With those sales the price loses 306.40 and confirms the break with a daily close below it.

- The week closes between 293 and 306, with the center near 299. But since speculators hold barely 10,583 long lots — against an all-time high of 144,732 — there is very little fuel for a collapse. It would be an orderly decline, step by step, not a crash.

### Bullish scenario — Probability: 21%

- A single trigger is enough to light the fuse: heavy rains on Wednesday the 29th over the drying zones, an inventory figure worse than expected, or a large round of roaster buying. With certified stocks at more than two-year lows, any of that news hits twice as hard.

- The funds are practically empty: 24,866 net long lots against an all-time high of 144,732. There is almost nobody left to push out of the market, but there is a great deal of room for them to come in. And when they have to come in, they come in running and all at the same time.

- The price recovers its 200-day moving average at 323.22 on volume and enters the 310-to-355-cent band, where historically almost no coffee has been traded. In that void the price finds no brake and can cover 15 or 20 cents in two or three days.

- The week closes between 324 and 342, with the center near 331 and a natural target at 330–335. It is the least probable of the three scenarios, but by far the fastest and most violent if it activates — whoever arrives late pays dearly.
