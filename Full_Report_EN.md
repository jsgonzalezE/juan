## 2. Fundamental analysis

### 2.1 Macroeconomic context

**The global coffee market is in a period of high volatility**, driven by a combination of macroeconomic tensions, logistical delays in Brazil, and growing climate risks associated with El Niño.

On **June 18, 2026**, the signing of a memorandum of understanding between the United States and Iran brought 60 days of temporary relief to energy prices. However, the **resumption of attacks on July 7** reactivated energy-sector tensions, consolidating expectations of **persistent inflation at the global level**.

The increase in initial margin requirements reduced market liquidity, triggering margin calls and forcing funds to close positions quickly, which amplified price movements. In addition, the arbitrage gap between Arabica and Robusta widened notably.

Although harvesting accelerated in July, the pace of Arabica sales and exports remains below the historical average.

Despite the delay, the rains did not affect total volume and **an exceptional harvest is expected in Brazil** that would guarantee a global surplus. Additionally, the mid-July exemption from the 25% tariff imposed by the U.S. on Brazilian instant coffee could boost its exports.

>
> On margins he explains the mechanism more clearly than the document: the increase in **New York exchange** requirements led many operators and funds to close positions *"because it became much more expensive to hold open positions"* — and that **makes movements more volatile**.

**Impact by region:**

- **Brazil:** may cause higher temperatures during winter, reducing the probability of frost; but during flowering it may cause very high temperatures that affect it considerably.
- **Vietnam:** below-average rainfall forced a marginal reduction in harvest projections.
- **Indonesia:** concern about the impact of dry weather during the development phase of the 27/28 crop.
- **Central America:** irregular rainfall patterns. **Colombia:** weather close to average, though El Niño could threaten the Mitaca harvest.

Generally, years with an active El Niño are positive for coffee production, but when the phenomenon intensifies and becomes very strong — as forecast — it can cause considerable damage to the harvests of the main producing countries. That is why monitoring its progress is essential to anticipate price movements.

In the short term, NOAA maintains its **El Niño Advisory with 97% persistence**, and for now there is no frost forecast for any producing region of Brazil. This may cause a liquidation of speculative longs held only as frost cover, as August advances without cold.

### 2.5 Harvest progress in Brazil

Brazil's Arabica harvest reached **63% as of July 24**, a jump of **9 points in a single week** (it had been at 54%), but still below the **70%** five-year average.

Brazil remains about 7 points behind, and the delay is concentrated precisely in **South of Minas (60% vs. 70% historical)** and **Cerrado (59% vs. 65%)**, the zones that define the country's Arabica volume.

That pace of +9 points weekly is the fastest of the season and shows that dry weather is allowing lost ground to be recovered. This leaves a market with a bearish or heavy-sideways bias, with short rebounds from some weather anomaly, and growing pressure as the big wave of new coffee arrives in late July and August.

For next week this works in favor of the price, giving it support: with inventories so low and no pending certifications, any news on weather, logistics or roaster buying hits harder, increasing volatility.

Such low certified stocks also reflect that sellers prefer the **physical market** — with better differentials — rather than delivering on the exchange, so it is not all genuine coffee scarcity in the world.

**If bags pending certification start to appear during the week, it would be a bearish signal for the price.**

### 2.7 Participant positioning

**Open interest** fell to **220,681 lots**, some 52,000 fewer than six weeks ago, and that money which left did not come back.

The funds stayed still: money managers closed with **24,866 net long lots**, almost identical to the 24,967 of the previous week, and speculators fell from 12,454 to **10,583**. Nobody is betting heavily on anything.

At the same time, producers and industry continue with a very small short position, only **−27,355 lots** when the ten-year minimum is −106,191, which means that whoever holds physical coffee **is not fixing price at these levels**. They are holding out.

A market with little money inside is a **light market: it moves more on less**. The base reading is a sideways week, with abrupt jumps due to lack of volume rather than real news.

With speculators at 10,583 net long against an all-time high of **144,732**, there are almost no longs to liquidate — little fuel to fall — and there is enormous room to buy if a trigger appears.

If a weather alert, an inventory figure or any supply noise occurs, it can set off a rapid rise, because the funds would have to rush in. If nothing happens, pressure from the Brazilian harvest keeps the ceiling in place and Arabica falls asleep in its range.

## 3. Technical analysis

### 3.1 Break of structure

Since October 2025 coffee had been making progressively lower highs — 338.25 in February, 319.50 in March, 306.25 in April, 297.20 in May, 269.40 in early June — the classic signature of a healthy downtrend.

The **rally to 357.00** took out all those highs at once, generating the **first higher high since October** of last year. Technically that is a **break of structure**: the medium-term downtrend is no longer in force. This is confirmed by the weekly MACD, which crossed upward.

Day to day, by contrast, the market is trapped: it has spent **three weeks oscillating between 306 and 357**, with the daily MACD crossed downward (histogram −2.87) and the RSI at 53.16, exactly neutral.

The combined reading of the three horizons is that of a market that broke its downtrend, rose too fast, and is now **catching its breath** before defining the next move. We are in a phase of energy accumulation, and what matters is not today's price but **which of the two edges of the range breaks first**.

## 6. Possible scenarios for next week

### 6.1 Neutral scenario — Probability: 43%

- The Brazilian harvest keeps running fast and there is no frost on the radar, but certified inventories at 311,317 bags with not a single bag pending certification keep the price from falling. **The two forces cancel each other out and neither wins.**
- There is very little money inside the market: open interest stood at 220,681 lots, some 52,000 fewer than six weeks ago, and the funds did not move. A market like this **moves in jumps, but without direction**.
- Coffee stays trapped between **306.40 and 323.22**, sitting right on top of its 200-day moving average. Every attempt to rise to 320–323 meets sellers, and every drop to 307–309 brings out a buyer. It is an even contest.
- **The week closes between 307 and 321, with the center near 314.** The rains looming for Wednesday the 29th give a one-day scare, the market digests it, and the fight to define the range is postponed another week.

### 6.2 Bearish scenario — Probability: 36%

- The big wave of new coffee arrives. Harvesting moves from 63% to around **72–75%** and the market finally begins to price in what is already a fact: Brazil's record harvest of **71.9 million bags** and a global Arabica surplus close to **9.5 million bags**.
- **Bags pending certification appear** after several weeks at zero. That is the key signal: it means physical coffee is coming back to the exchange and it removes the only real support the price had.
- With no frost forecast, funds that remained long only as insurance against cold release the position as August approaches with nothing to fear. With those sales the price **loses 306.40** and confirms the break with a daily close below it.
- **The week closes between 293 and 306, with the center near 299.** But since speculators hold barely 10,583 long lots — against an all-time high of 144,732 — there is very little fuel for a collapse. It would be an **orderly decline, step by step, not a crash**.

### 6.3 Bullish scenario — Probability: 21%

- A single trigger is enough to light the fuse: heavy rains on Wednesday the 29th over the drying zones, an inventory figure worse than expected, or a large round of roaster buying. With certified stocks at more than two-year lows, **any of that news hits twice as hard**.
- The funds are practically empty: 24,866 net long lots against an all-time high of 144,732. There is almost nobody left to push out of the market, but there is a great deal of room for them to come in. **And when they have to come in, they come in running and all at the same time.**
- The price recovers its 200-day moving average at **323.22** on volume and enters the 310-to-355-cent band, where historically almost no coffee has been traded. In that void the price finds no brake and **can cover 15 or 20 cents in two or three days**.
- **The week closes between 324 and 342, with the center near 331** and a natural target at 330–335. It is the least probable of the three scenarios, but by far the fastest and most violent if it activates: **whoever arrives late pays dearly**.
